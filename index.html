About

I'm a first-year PhD student at Northeastern University, where my research sits at the intersection of aerospace materials science, advanced manufacturing, and computational modeling. My current work focuses on how miscible polymers interdiffuse at interfaces — a fundamental process governing bond strength in laser-assisted automated fiber placement (AFP).

Before my PhD, I completed a BS in Aerospace Engineering at Virginia Tech (May 2025) and spent three consecutive terms at NASA Langley's Advanced Materials & Processing Branch across three research projects: SUMAC (sustainable thermoplastic composites), TTT (machine learning for bondline NDE), and OATMEAL (laser ablation and polymer healing), where I remain active.

I also led the printing team for Virginia Tech's 3D Printed Aircraft Competition team for three years, placing 2nd nationally in 2023 and 2025, and winning Most Innovative Design in 2025.

Research

Polymer diffusion & laser-induced surface engineering

Developing predictive models linking polymer interdiffusion with laser–material interactions for controlled ablation in thermoplastic AFP. Writing first-author manuscript for Composites Part A.

OATMEAL Project · NASA Langley · 2025–present

Machine learning for composite bondline NDE

Trained SVMs and Gaussian process regression models to improve in-situ ultrasonic bondline thickness measurements during autoclave cure by 15%. Co-authored two papers under review.

TTT Project · NASA Langley · 2024

Sustainable flax fiber thermoplastic composites

Investigated flax fiber reinforced thermoplastics for advanced air mobility applications. Synthesized PVA hydrogels via freeze-thaw cycling; characterized resins by TGA, DSC, and DMA.
